SteelSeries Rival 3 Wireless Gen 2: UK buying guide

Research-based product guide · Updated 2 November 2026

Quick verdict: a flexible wireless mouse, with one clear trade-off

Short answer

The strongest reason to shortlist this SteelSeries mouse is connection flexibility. Its 2.4GHz receiver is the sensible mode for gaming, while Bluetooth provides an easy route to a second compatible device. The main decision is whether you prefer replaceable AAA batteries over an internal rechargeable battery.

It makes the most sense for a buyer who values long intervals between battery changes, wants a conventional six-button layout and is comfortable with a mouse that is not marketed as an ultra-light model. If minimum moving weight, USB charging or a large bank of side buttons is essential, compare alternatives before ordering.

Reasons to shortlist it

  • 2.4GHz receiver and Bluetooth modes
  • Can run from one or two AAA batteries
  • Six-button layout for gaming and normal desktop use
  • Onboard settings supported through SteelSeries GG

Reasons to compare first

  • No built-in USB-rechargeable battery
  • One battery reduces weight but also changes battery endurance
  • Two left-side buttons do not make it an MMO-style mouse
  • Exact Gen 2 listing must be checked carefully

Who should consider this SteelSeries mouse?

Match it to your setup

1

The PC gamer who also uses a laptop

Leave the USB receiver with the gaming PC and use Bluetooth with a compatible laptop or tablet. This is convenient, although changing devices still requires using the mode switch rather than a dedicated top button.

2

The buyer who dislikes charging cables

AAA power avoids plugging the mouse in to recharge. Keep spare batteries available, or use rechargeable AAA cells with a separate charger. SteelSeries explicitly supports rechargeable AAA batteries.

3

The player who wants a familiar button layout

Six buttons cover the standard clicks, wheel, sensitivity control and two left-side thumb inputs. That is enough for many games, but players who map numerous commands should compare a mouse designed around extra side controls.

Shape warning: a specification sheet cannot tell you whether a shell will fit your hand. Compare the published dimensions with a mouse you already find comfortable and check the retailer’s return terms.

One AAA or two: choose weight versus endurance

The key setup decision

SteelSeries allows the mouse to run with either one or two AAA batteries. The company says removing one battery saves 10g. This makes the power system adjustable, but the lighter option also gives up some of the endurance available from two cells.

  • Choose one AAA if reducing moving weight matters more than the longest possible interval between battery changes.
  • Choose two AAA batteries if endurance and fewer battery swaps matter more than the added weight.
  • Choose rechargeable AAA cells if you want to reduce disposable-battery use; remember that charging happens outside the mouse.

Whichever route you choose, do not mix battery types or old and new cells. Follow the safety and installation guidance supplied with the product.

SteelSeries Rival 3 Wireless Gen 2 FAQs

Common buying questions

Does the SteelSeries Rival 3 Wireless Gen 2 use Bluetooth?

Yes. SteelSeries lists both 2.4GHz wireless through the USB receiver and Bluetooth. The receiver mode is the sensible default for gaming, while Bluetooth can be useful for a second compatible device.

Does it recharge by USB?

No. The mouse runs on one or two AAA batteries. Rechargeable AAA batteries are supported, but they need a separate compatible charger.

Is Gen 2 the same as the original Rival 3 Wireless?

No. Gen 2 is a newer model with changes that SteelSeries says include improved 2.4GHz battery life, reduced click latency and 100% PTFE feet. Check the full listing title before ordering.

Can it save settings?

SteelSeries says settings including DPI, polling rate, button mappings, macros and selected wireless features can be stored on the mouse through SteelSeries GG. Review the current support documentation for any software or platform limitations.
